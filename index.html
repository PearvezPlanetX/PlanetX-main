How to Spot Moving Objects Across 102 IR Bands

Every six months, the SPHEREx all-sky survey captures the entire celestial sphere across 102 near-infrared bands (0.75–5.0 µm). Distant stars remain stationary, while comets, asteroids, brown dwarfs, and Planet X candidates shift position between epochs.

01
Choose a Sky Sector

Choose a known target or enter RA/Dec coordinates to inspect real FITS data. Green sectors on the map are also available.

02
Blink Epochs or View Dipole Difference

Press Spacebar (or click Blink Epochs) to flip between 6-month snapshots. Switch to Dipole Difference Map to highlight moving sources as a crimson/cyan pair.

03
Inspect the 102-Band Spectrum

Click any moving dot to lock the reticle and plot its infrared flux curve. Scrub the band slider—icy Planet X / brown dwarf candidates show a methane dip near 3.3 µm.

04
Flag Discoveries or Clear Sectors

Click Flag Moving Candidate to archive the coordinates in your Candidate Log tab, or click Mark Sector Clear to return to the full sky.
